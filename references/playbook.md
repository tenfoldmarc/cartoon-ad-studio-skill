# The viral cartoon ad playbook

Built from a breakdown of top-spending cartoon video ads in the Meta Ad Library (Nov 2026): what they say, how they are
cut, what they look like. Two formats did almost all of the spend. Pick one per ad.

| | Format A: "Impossible What-If" explainer | Format B: Pixar family story |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 60 s (cutdown) to 2 min | 60 to 90 s for a direct-response ad (the originals run 2 to 5 min) |
| Voice | calm, quick documentary narrator, ~180 words per minute | warm first-person storyteller, ~120 wpm, real pauses after emotional lines |
| Look | one faceless everyman mannequin, huge 3D numbers, scale jumps | a consistent family cast, props that carry emotion, before/after scenes that mirror |
| Shots | one every ~2.5 s (24 shots for 60 s) | one every 2.5 to 7 s (about 30 for 70 s) |
| Best for | an offer you can tie to a surprising fact | an offer that gives someone their time, family or freedom back |

---

## Format A: the "Impossible What-If" explainer

### Script skeleton (9 beats)
1. **Absurd but true hypothetical, tied to the viewer's pain word.** "If you could fold your paycheck in half 42 times,
   it would reach the Moon."
2. **Escalation with concrete numbers.** 7 folds = a notebook, 17 = taller than a house, 30 = space, 42 = the Moon.
3. **"There's just one catch."** You can't fold paper that many times.
4. **Bridge: the catch mirrors the pain.** "...which is also true of your paycheck. It doesn't double. It shows up."
5. **Reframe: the problem isn't X, it's Y.** "The answer was never to stretch one paycheck further. It was to stop
   relying on just one."
6. **Offer:** what it is, when, free or price, what it does for them.
7. **Objection stack** (long version only): "You don't need code, a business, followers..." ending on a joke callback.
8. **Anti-hype line** (long version only): "This isn't a promise you'll get rich overnight..."
9. **CTA with a callback to the hook:** "No folding required. Click the button to save your seat."
Ratio: in the 60 s version the offer does not appear until about 0:44. Roughly 70% entertainment, 30% pitch. People
watch for the fact and stay for the turn.

### Visual rules
1. **Every line gets a literal picture of its metaphor.** Weekdays become concrete blocks pinning him to the bed, then
   marionette strings. The boss is a giant shadowy suit. Monday is a black monolith in a lightning storm.
2. **Key numbers become huge extruded 3D type inside the scene** (7, 17, 42, "7 HOURS LATE", the event date). The number
   is the shot.
3. **Faceless everyman mannequin:** a smooth matte beige-grey figure with no clear age, race or style, the same figure
   in every scene. Anyone can see themselves in him (and it hides face drift between clips).
4. **Scale contrast:** a tiny human next to enormous objects.
5. **Lighting:** low-key and dark, one warm practical light against cool blues. Rendered, not "AI shiny".
6. **Jump scale constantly:** desk, house, orbit, Moon, back to the desk.
7. **Opening frame = the payoff image** (the paper tower already smashing through the ceiling).

### Fill-in template
> "If you could [impossible hack that fixes PAIN], [startling true result]."
> [3 or 4 escalating real numbers, each one a 3D object on screen]
> "There's just one catch. [the real-world reason it fails]... which is also true of your [PAIN WORD]."
> "Most people don't actually hate [X]. They hate [deeper Y]."
> "That's why [OFFER: free / date / what you'll get]."
> "You don't need [3 objections, the last one a callback joke to the hook]."
> "[Callback to the hook]. Click the button to [low-friction ask]."

Niche examples (**check every fact before use**: the hook falls apart if the fact is wrong):
- Dental implants: "If you flossed every day for 40 years..." and the catch is the bone loss you can't undo.
- Real estate: "If you saved $500 a month for a down payment..." and the catch is prices rising faster than you save.
- Fitness 40+: "If you walked to the Moon at 10,000 steps a day..." and the catch is it doesn't move the scale after 40.

## Format B: the Pixar family story

### Script skeleton (8 to 10 beats)
1. **Confession hook with a specific number, ideally one that contradicts itself.** "My wife almost divorced me after I
   got my dream job." / "I missed forty-one bedtimes last year. I counted."
2. **What it cost, in small specific details.** "Noah had 11 games that season. I made 2." The storybook knock on the
   office door. "Five more minutes, baby." It was never five minutes.
3. **Failed fixes, which answer the viewer's objections before they come up.** He asked for flexible hours ("nothing
   changed"); she hired an editor ("$1,500 a month, I couldn't afford it past month two"); the cheap apps all look the
   same; posting less ("my business noticed before I did").
4. **A believable messenger.** A brother who looks ten years younger, a mom from the running group.
5. **The skeptic speaks.** "I'm not doing another one of these." / "I can't even code." Reply: "Neither can I."
6. **Proof, if you have real proof** (a result on a phone screen, only numbers you can show). No proof? Skip the beat.
   Never invent it.
7. **The mechanism, made small.** "You drop your raw clips in a folder. It keeps the best take, cuts the dead air..."
8. **Offer details told as story** (what, when, how long, what they walk away with).
9. **The ending repeats the opening scenes, now fixed.** He drives the kids to school; the table is laid for four; at
   seven-thirty she's the one knocking on the door.
10. **Thesis line + CTA.** "I didn't stop making videos. I just stopped editing them." "Tap the button to get yours."

### Visual rules
- **A consistent cast.** Same faces and clothes across every shot. Make a character sheet per person first and pass it
  as a reference to every still. Without that the format falls apart.
- **Props carry the emotion:** an empty folding chair with his jacket on it, the table set for 3 and later for 4, a
  calendar with days crossed out, the glow of a laptop in a dark room.
- **Before and after shots mirror each other:** the same office door, the same dinner table from the same angle. The
  ending lands because the viewer already saw the "before".
- **Gentle motion:** small acting, slow push-ins, real pauses in the voice with music underneath.

### Worked example: "Forty-one bedtimes" (Format B, 66 s of voice + 3 s end card, 31 shots)
> I missed forty-one bedtimes last year. I counted.
> I'd film a one-minute video for my business at four o'clock. Then edit it until midnight. Cutting every "um."
> Typing captions one word at a time.
> Lily would knock on my office door with her storybook. "Five more minutes, baby." It was never five minutes.
> I tried hiring an editor. Fifteen hundred dollars a month. I couldn't afford it past month two.
> I tried the caption apps. Everything looked like everyone else's. I tried posting less. My business noticed before
> I did.
> Then a mom from my running group sent me a reel and said, "[Product] edited this."
> I said, "[Product]? The AI? I can't even code." She said, "Neither can I."
> You drop your raw clips in a folder. It watches every take, keeps the best one, cuts the dead air, and adds the
> captions and effects in your own style. It can even post them for you.
> Now I film after school pickup. It edits while we make dinner. And at seven-thirty, I'm the one knocking on her door.
> I didn't stop making videos. I just stopped editing them.
> It's called [Product]. Tap the button to get yours.

| Beat | Line |
|---|---|
| Confession hook + number | "I missed forty-one bedtimes last year. I counted." |
| What it cost, small details | four o'clock to midnight, every "um", the storybook knock, "five more minutes" |
| Failed fixes = objections | an editor at $1,500/mo, caption apps that look the same, posting less |
| Messenger + skeptic | a mom from the running group: "I can't even code." "Neither can I." |
| Mechanism, made simple | raw clips in a folder; best take, dead air, captions, effects, your style |
| Ending mirrors the opening | films after pickup, it edits during dinner, SHE knocks on the door |
| Thesis | "I didn't stop making videos. I just stopped editing them." |
| Offer + CTA | name + tap the button |
Claims check that went with it: every product claim matches what the product really does; the narrator is a
fictional character, not a customer; no follower, view or income results are given, only time back.

## Editing and sound (both formats)
- **Hard cuts only.** No transitions, zooms or whooshes. One line of the script = one shot; cuts land on phrase breaks.
- **Captions:** small white sans-serif, 1 to 3 words at a time, centered in the middle of the frame, no box, no
  highlight colors. They stay quiet because the picture does the work.
- **Audio:** the voice dominates, with a constant music bed under it (the pauses are not silent). About -14 LUFS.
  Few sound effects: a knock, a ding, a click, each where the story asks for it.
- **End card:** a 3 second brand card (offer, name, link). The only branded moment.

## The principles (work in any niche)
1. **Make the ad look like content.** Lead with a fact or a story, show the product late, don't show a guru's face.
   (The advertisers studied even name their page after the audience's desire, like a tips page.)
2. **Start the hook from the pain word.** The hypothetical or the confession contains the exact word the avatar already
   uses about their problem (paycheck, Monday, bedtime, editing).
3. **Hypothetical, then the catch, then a reframe** (Format A). The offer solves the reframed problem.
4. **Specific numbers everywhere.** 42 folds, 11 games and he made 2, $1,500 a month, forty-one bedtimes.
5. **One literal picture per line.** If a line can't be pictured, rewrite the line.
6. **An everyman the viewer projects onto:** a faceless mannequin (A) or a family that looks like the viewer's (B).
7. **Build objection handling into the story:** failed attempts, a skeptic who speaks up, "you don't need...".
8. **End on a callback to the hook.** "No folding required." "I'm the one knocking on her door."
9. **Make the ask easy:** free, a date, one tap.
10. **Build each concept once, then cut versions:** a full version, a 60 s cutdown, two hooks on the same body.

## Honesty rules (non-negotiable)
- Characters are fictional and presented as a story, never as a customer testimonial.
- No invented results, screenshots, reviews, follower counts or income claims. A number about the product (price,
  dates, features) comes from the sales page or the user.
- Every product claim in the script must be something the product really does (claims check in SCRIPT.md).
- Health, money and legal niches: no promises of outcomes; check the ad platform's policy for the niche.

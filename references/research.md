# Step 2: sales page to avatar research

Input: the sales page (a link, a saved page, screenshots or pasted text). Read all of it, top to bottom, including
FAQ, guarantee, testimonials and fine print. If it is a link, fetch it; if the page blocks fetching, ask for
screenshots (full-page) or the text.

Write `<project>/research.md` with exactly these sections. Keep every line short and in the avatar's own words where
the page gives them. Quote the page for anything factual.

```markdown
# Research: <offer name>

## The offer (facts only, quoted from the page)
- What it is:
- Price / free / trial:
- Dates or deadline (if any):
- What the buyer gets:
- Guarantee:
- Link:

## The avatar
One line: who they are, what they do all day, what they want. (e.g. "Mom who runs a small online coaching business
and posts talking-head reels to get clients.")

## Dream outcomes (what life looks like after)
- 4 to 6 concrete, visual outcomes. Things you can film: "film after school pickup", "be there for bedtime".

## Pain points (what it costs them now)
- 4 to 6 concrete, visual pains with numbers where the page has them: "editing until midnight", "an editor at
  $1,500 a month".

## Pain words
- The exact words the avatar uses for the problem (the hook starts from one of these): editing, Monday, paycheck...

## What they already tried (and why it failed)
- 3 failed fixes. These become the "failed fixes" beat and answer objections before they come up.

## Objections
- 3 to 5 reasons they would not buy ("I can't code", "it'll look like everyone else's", "no time to learn").

## Allowed claims
- Every claim the ad may make, each one backed by a line on the page. Nothing else goes in the script.

## Not allowed
- Results, numbers or testimonials the page does not prove. Anything the product does not really do.

## Format pick
A (what-if explainer) or B (family story), and one sentence why. Ideas for the hook: 3 options, each starting from a
pain word, each with a specific number.
```

Rules:
- Specific beats general. "41 bedtimes" beats "less family time". If the page has no number, the story can use a
  personal-scale number the character counted (bedtimes missed, games missed), which is fiction inside a story, never
  a product result.
- If the page is thin, ask the user 3 short questions (who buys, the #1 pain, the #1 outcome) instead of guessing.
- Show the user the research in chat (short) and get a yes or edits before writing the script.
